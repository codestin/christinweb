# Why Editing Sucks and What To Do About It

*Originally published on WriteofPassage.school in February 2023*

This essay required 20,000 words and countless hours of conversation to distill into its current form. Writing and editing felt like "navigating a dark forest," stumbling down "faint and meandering trails of thought."

Editing creates emotional wounds. Writers receive feedback comments that feel like accusations of flaws. Worse, feedback can trigger existential doubt: "Your friends find out that you're a complete fraud, as lost as anyone else."

**The core insight:** Publishing isn't editing's ultimate reward. Rather, **editing can be a mystical act toward liberation** and self-discovery.

## We Identify with Our Words

Drawing on Buddhist philosophy, we suffer because we attach false identity to the five aggregates (form, feelings, perceptions, mental processes, consciousness). Writers make the same mistake—subconsciously associating their words with who they are.

The ego operates like a cat chasing a laser pointer: "The cat is certain that the laser beam is prey, and gets stressed out chasing after it." When essays disappoint us, we believe *we* have disappointed—perpetuating the false identity trap.

## How We Avoid Pain

Writers dodge editing through subtle avoidance. Instead of stopping completely, they shift toward "short pieces at a rapid cadence" (tweets, atomic essays, newsletters) rather than tackling difficult, convoluted essays.

This relates to the concept of "Ugh Fields"—unconscious flinching from serious problems. An Ugh Field is "by definition, cannot be seen by the person under its influence." The ego uses Ugh Fields to protect us from editing pain while we chase metrics and audience-building.

## The Problem Leads to the Solution

The essay reframes editing's purpose. The word "essay" originally meant "to test the quality of." Beyond testing ideas, editing tests **the quality of our awareness.**

Turning inward to face editing's turmoil "can, over time, liberate us from the ego." Kurt Vonnegut advised students: tearing up a poem and discarding it teaches that "You have experienced becoming, learned a lot about what's inside you, and you have made your soul grow."

## Conclusion

"By working through the process and maintaining awareness, we become a little less lost, and our wounds from the little soldiers start to heal."

Editing, when approached mindfully, reveals ego's tricks and facilitates genuine self-knowledge.
